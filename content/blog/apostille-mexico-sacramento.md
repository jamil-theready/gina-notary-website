## What is an Apostille and Why Does Mexico Require One?

An apostille is a certification that authenticates the signature and seal of a public official on a document. It ensures the document's legal validity in foreign countries. This international certification system streamlines the process, replacing the older, more complex diplomatic legalization steps.

Mexico is a signatory to the Hague Apostille Convention. This means countries like Mexico accept apostilles issued by other member countries, including the United States, as valid forms of authentication. Without an apostille, your California issued document holds no legal weight in Mexico. It is a crucial step for almost any official document crossing borders between these nations. I understand the nuances of securing an apostille for Mexico from Sacramento.

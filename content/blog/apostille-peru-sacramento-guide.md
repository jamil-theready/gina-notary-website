## What is an Apostille and why is it needed for Peru?

An apostille is a certification that authenticates the signature and seal of a public official on a document. It is a streamlined form of legalization recognized by countries participating in the Hague Apostille Convention. Both the United States and Peru are members of this convention. This means that instead of a lengthy embassy legalization process, a single apostille certificate makes your U.S. document valid for use in Peru.

Without an apostille, your U.S. birth certificate, power of attorney, or other official document will not be accepted by Peruvian authorities. This certification confirms that the notary public or government official who signed your document is legitimate. It ensures your documents hold legal weight across international borders.

## What documents typically need an Apostille for Peru?

Many types of documents require an apostille when destined for Peru. These often include personal documents, academic records, and legal or business papers.

Here are some common examples:

*   **Personal Documents:** Birth certificates, marriage certificates, death certificates, divorce decrees.
*   **Academic Documents:** Diplomas, transcripts, letters of enrollment.
*   **Legal Documents:** Power of attorney forms, affidavits, wills, court orders.
*   **Business Documents:** Articles of incorporation, certificates of good standing, commercial invoices.

Each document must be an original or a certified copy issued by the appropriate authority. For instance, a birth certificate needs to be a certified copy from the county recorder.
